Morzine has a reputation as the British-friendly gateway to the Portes du Soleil, and that's mostly fair. What that reputation undersells is how genuinely good the skiing is, how much character the town has retained, and how easy it is to spend a week here without ever doing the same run twice.

Here's what we'd actually do with a week in Morzine.

## Ski the Portes du Soleil properly

The Portes du Soleil is 600+ kilometres of linked pistes across 12 resorts straddling the French-Swiss border. Most visitors stay on the Morzine-Les Gets side, which is excellent — but you're missing the point if you don't venture further.

Head over to Avoriaz on a sunny morning (the village is car-free and feels like another planet), then drop down into Châtel for lunch. The classic Swiss-side day is the route via Les Crosets to Champéry — quieter pistes, dramatic scenery, and the chance to say you skied to Switzerland for lunch.

For something different, the Hauts Forts black runs above Avoriaz are some of the most underrated descents in France. Steep, long, and almost always empty on weekdays.

## The lunch spots locals actually use

Skip the obvious on-piste self-services. **Chez Nannon** above Les Lindarets is the genuine article — a tiny mountain restaurant doing proper Savoyard food. **La Cremaillère** at the top of the Pleney lift is reliably good and has the views. For something simple and brilliant, **Le Plan des Lacs** does a tartiflette that has ruined every other tartiflette for us.

In town, **L'Atelier** does serious modern French food in an unfussy setting. **La Chamade** is the splash-out dinner. And if you want a quiet local spot, **La Grange** does fondue the way it's meant to be done.

## Après that isn't a meat market

Morzine has a deserved reputation for late nights, but it's also possible to have a civilised drink. The **Cavern** is the loud option (and good fun if that's what you want). **Le Tremplin** by the main lift is the genuine après spot — outdoor terrace, live music most afternoons, locals and visitors mixed.

For something quieter, **Bec Jaune** is a brewery doing properly good craft beer. Worth a stop even if you're not staying for the night.

## When you don't want to ski

The natural hot springs at **Les Bains de Morzine** are a perfect rest-day stop — outdoor pools surrounded by snow, exactly what tired legs need.

The **husky sledding** at Plateau de Loex is touristy but legitimately fun. Book ahead.

For non-skiing companions or kids, the **Indiana'Ventures** snowshoe tours run guided routes through quieter forest trails — good way to see the landscape without lift passes.

And if you've got a car, **Annecy** is an hour away. The lake town in winter is gorgeous and worth a day if the weather turns.

## The thing nobody tells you

Morzine sits at a relatively low altitude (1000m), which means in poor snow years the village runs can be patchy. The lifts to Avoriaz (1800m) are reliable — just don't expect to ski back to your front door in March. Build that into your planning and you won't be disappointed.

Morzine rewards people who explore. Stay too close to the centre and you'll have a fine holiday. Push out into the wider Portes du Soleil and you'll have a great one.
