Ski holidays have got noticeably more expensive over the past few seasons. Lift passes, mountain restaurants, accommodation, transfers — it has all gone up. But the price rises haven't been uniform, and the conventional wisdom about where to save isn't always right.

Here's an honest breakdown of where the money actually goes on a ski holiday, and where you can sensibly cut without ruining the week.

## A realistic week's budget

For a couple skiing in a mid-range French resort, mid-season, here's roughly what you're looking at in 2026 (per person, six days skiing):

- **Flights:** £150–£300
- **Transfers:** £80–£150
- **Accommodation (catered chalet or apartment):** £600–£1200
- **Lift pass (6 days):** £280–£380
- **Equipment hire:** £140–£220
- **Lessons or guiding:** £0–£400+ (group lessons average £180–£250 for the week; private lessons run higher)
- **Food and drink:** £350–£700 (the variable one)
- **Insurance:** £40–£80

That's a realistic range of **£1,640–£3,430 per person** for a week. The spread tells you something important: most of the variation isn't in the unavoidable costs, it's in the choices.

## Where prices rose the most

Three categories have seen the steepest increases:

**Mountain restaurants** have gone up significantly. A modest lunch on the slopes that was €25 a few years ago is now €40+ in many resorts. Swiss resorts are particularly painful.

**Lift passes** in major linked areas have outpaced inflation. The Three Valleys, Paradiski, and Espace Killy passes have all crept up year on year.

**Last-minute accommodation** has become noticeably more expensive. The flexibility premium that used to exist has shrunk; booking early now saves you meaningfully more than it used to.

## Where to genuinely save

**Book early.** This is by far the single biggest lever. Six-month-out chalet bookings often run 20-30% cheaper than three-month-out. Early-bird lift pass deals are real and worth taking.

**Eat lunch off-piste.** A packed lunch in your backpack saves €30+ per person per day. Even better: ski down to a village for lunch where prices are half what they are mid-mountain. In Méribel, dropping into Méribel-Village; in Verbier, skiing down to Le Châble; in Morzine, the village itself.

**Travel mid-week to mid-week.** Saturday-to-Saturday is the most expensive changeover. Sunday-to-Sunday or Monday-to-Monday can be 15-20% cheaper for flights, transfers, and accommodation — and the lifts are far quieter on Sundays.

**Go second week of January or mid-March.** Pre-half-term January and post-Easter March are the genuinely cheap weeks. Snow is usually excellent in January; March is variable but often glorious. Avoid them only if you specifically need school holidays.

**Stay in a less famous resort.** Les Gets instead of Méribel, Champagny-en-Vanoise instead of Courchevel, La Tzoumaz instead of Verbier — same lift area, dramatically lower cost.

## Where not to save

**Don't skimp on lessons.** This is the easiest place to think you can save and the worst place to actually save. A poorly-matched group lesson at half price isn't a saving, it's a wasted week. Pay for the right lesson with the right instructor.

**Don't cheap out on boots.** Rented boots that don't fit will ruin your week faster than anything else. If the rental shop has fitted you something painful, go back and demand a swap, or pay extra for premium-fit rentals.

**Don't skip insurance.** A serious skiing injury without insurance is financially ruinous. The £50–£80 for a winter-sports policy is non-negotiable.

**Don't drive 10 hours to save £100.** Tired drivers in bad weather end up in ditches. The savings versus flying-plus-transfer rarely justify the time and risk unless you're going for a fortnight or have lots of kit.

## The thing that saves you the most money

It's boring, and it's this: book early, in a less famous resort, mid-week to mid-week, in a non-school-holiday week, with lessons matched to your actual level.

Do those five things and you'll spend half what someone going to Méribel half-term will spend, and you'll probably have a better week.

## The reality check

A good ski holiday isn't cheap. But "good" doesn't require Méribel-in-half-term prices. We've taught families having brilliant weeks in Les Gets on tight budgets, and miserable weeks in Verbier on huge ones. Where you spend matters more than how much you spend.
