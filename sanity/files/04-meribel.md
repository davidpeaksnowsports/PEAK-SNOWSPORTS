Méribel has a slightly awkward reputation: too British, too expensive, too in-the-middle. None of those are quite fair. What Méribel actually is, is the natural centre of the largest linked ski area on the planet, and a village that — for all its development — has held on to its wooden-chalet charm better than most.

Here's how to make the most of it.

## Use the position properly

The whole point of Méribel is that you can ski into Courchevel one day and into Val Thorens the next. Most people don't. They stick around the Méribel bowl, which is fine but underuses the lift pass.

Spend a full day in Val Thorens — it's higher, snowier, and the skiing is properly serious. Spend another in Courchevel for the contrast (the pistes are immaculately groomed, the lunches expensive, the people-watching unmatched). Save your Méribel-only days for poor visibility or tired legs; the home bowl is sheltered and easier to navigate when it's snowing hard.

The link via Mont Vallon is the most beautiful in the Three Valleys and the one to do on a clear day. Get there early before the queues build.

## Pistes worth knowing about

The **Pic Bleu** chair accesses some of the best red runs in Méribel — empty most mornings because it's slightly off the main flow. The **Mont du Vallon** drop is dramatic and stays in good condition late into the season.

For genuinely quiet skiing, the **St Martin de Belleville** side (over the back of Méribel) is consistently overlooked. The pistes are cruisy, the village is gorgeous, and **La Bouitte** (two Michelin stars) is there for the once-in-a-lifetime lunch if budget allows.

## Lunch — where actual skiers eat

**La Ferme** on the way to Mottaret is the local classic. Family-run, real Savoyard food, reservations essential in peak weeks. **Les Pierres Plates** at the top of the Saulire lift has the views and reliably good food without the silly prices of the Courchevel side.

For a proper experience, **La Bouitte** in St Marcel (just below St Martin) is worth the trek and the wallet if you want to do one extraordinary lunch. Book weeks ahead.

In Méribel itself, **L'Arpasson** is the picturesque on-piste lunch (don't miss the ride down on the toboggan). **Le Refuge** in Méribel-Village does excellent fondue at sensible prices.

## Après and evenings

**La Folie Douce** at the top of Saulire is what it is — loud, expensive, on-mountain dancing in ski boots. Go once, have the experience. **Le Rond Point** at the bottom of the home run is the British après institution. Loud, fun, busy from 4pm.

For something more grown-up, the bar at **Le Grand Coeur** is quiet and proper. **Jack's** does decent cocktails. And in Méribel-Village, **Lodge du Village** is the relaxed local-feeling option.

## Non-skiing days

The Olympic ice rink (Méribel hosted ice hockey in 1992) is open to the public and properly fun. **Paragliding** off Tougnete on a clear day is unforgettable. And the **Méribel cinema** screens films in English, which sounds boring until you're three days into a snowstorm.

## The honest take

Méribel isn't cheap. The food is dearer than Morzine, the lessons cost more than Les Gets, and a week here adds up. What you're paying for is access — to the best linked ski area in the world, with a village that's pleasant rather than purpose-built ugly. For most people, once a year, that's worth it.
