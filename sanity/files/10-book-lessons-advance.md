Every January, we have the same conversation about a hundred times. Someone walks into the ski school office on the Saturday afternoon they arrive, asks about lessons starting Monday, and looks crestfallen when they're told the only available slots are 8am, with whichever instructor happens to be free.

The good lessons in good resorts in peak weeks are booked out by October. This isn't a marketing line — it's an operational reality, and most visitors don't understand it until they've been caught out by it.

Here's why advance booking matters more than most people realise.

## The instructor pool is finite

A ski resort has a fixed number of qualified instructors. There's no surge capacity — when half-term hits and demand triples, the same instructors are working the same hours. The difference is just which ones get booked first.

In a typical mid-sized resort, the highly-rated, fluent-English-speaking, full-ISIA-qualified instructors number in the dozens, not hundreds. In peak weeks, they're booked solid. The people booking in November get them. The people booking in January don't.

## What "fully booked" actually means

When a ski school tells you they're "fully booked" in February half-term, what they often mean is: they're fully booked with their best instructors. They can probably still take you, but you'll be allocated to whoever's available — which might be a trainee, a newly-qualified instructor, or someone covering outside their usual age group or level.

This isn't dishonest. They genuinely have capacity. But the gap between "the best instructor in the school" and "whoever's spare on Tuesday morning" is enormous, and you don't know which one you're getting if you book late.

Early bookers get to request specific instructors by name. Late bookers take what they're given.

## Group lessons fill predictably

Group lessons in particular operate on tight capacity. A typical group is 6-8 people. Once a group is full, that's it — they can't squeeze in a ninth person without ruining the lesson for the existing eight.

Popular levels (low intermediate, high beginner) fill first because that's where most skiers cluster. By the time you're booking three weeks out for half-term, "Level 3 — comfortable on blue runs" is gone in every school in the resort. You're either being put into Level 2 (too easy) or Level 4 (too hard) or skipping group lessons entirely.

## Private lessons fill even faster

Private one-to-one lessons with named, requested instructors are the first thing to disappear from booking calendars. Some long-standing instructors are booked 18 months out by repeat clients who return every year.

If you want a specific instructor — particularly one recommended to you, or one you've used before — book them as soon as you've decided your dates. Even before you've booked flights. Instructors will hold dates against deposits well in advance.

## The off-peak exception

If you're booking for a non-school-holiday week — early December, mid-January (after the first week), early February (before half-term), mid-March — the urgency is lower. Two or three months ahead is usually fine.

For Christmas/New Year, February half-term, and Easter, you should be booking by September or October.

## What to book first

The order most people get wrong:
1. Flights
2. Accommodation
3. Lift passes
4. Lessons
5. Transfers

The order that actually works better:
1. **Lessons** (locks in the best instructor and shapes your week)
2. **Accommodation** (now you can match location to where lessons start)
3. **Flights** (now you know your transfer day requirements)
4. **Transfers and lift passes** (admin items that book late just fine)

This sounds inverted but it's right. Lessons are the most capacity-constrained item with the biggest quality variance. Lock them in first. Everything else has more flexibility.

## The smart move

If you know roughly when you're skiing — even if not the exact week — contact ski schools early and ask about their booking patterns. A good school will tell you honestly: "Christmas is filling up, half-term has limited availability, we have flexibility in March." That information is gold for planning.

Many schools also offer flexible booking — you reserve an instructor's time with a deposit, and the exact lesson types can be confirmed closer to the date. This is the way to lock in quality without committing every detail months out.

## The bottom line

Ski lessons aren't like restaurant reservations where you can usually walk in and find something acceptable. They're more like seats at a specific concert: when they're gone, they're gone, and the alternative options are meaningfully worse. Book early, get the right instructor, and the rest of the holiday builds itself around a good foundation.
