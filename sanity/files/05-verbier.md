Verbier divides opinion. To freeriders, it's hallowed ground — the off-piste capital of Europe, home to the Xtreme finals, a place where the lift system feels almost incidental to what the mountain actually offers. To everyone else, it can feel intimidating, expensive, and a bit smug.

The truth is Verbier rewards people who know how to use it. Here's how.

## The skiing — and what you're really paying for

The lift-served terrain in Verbier is decent but not extraordinary. What makes Verbier exceptional is the off-piste: the **Vallon d'Arbi**, **Mont Gelé**, **Stairway to Heaven**, the bowls under **Mont Fort**. This is some of the best lift-accessible off-piste in the Alps.

If you're not a confident off-piste skier — and most visitors aren't — hire a guide for at least one day. It transforms the holiday. A good guide takes you to terrain you'd never find alone, in safer conditions than you'd ski alone, and shows you a mountain you'd otherwise miss completely.

For on-piste skiing, the **4 Vallées** linked area gives you genuine variety. Head to **Veysonnaz** for long, fast cruising reds. **Thyon** is quieter and good for less confident skiers. The link is fiddly and weather-dependent, so check before committing to a full day across.

## Mont Fort — the obligatory pilgrimage

Take the cable car to the top of **Mont Fort** (3330m) at least once. The 360-degree view takes in the Matterhorn and Mont Blanc on a clear day. The piste down is a genuine black — narrow, steep at the top, and one of those runs that feels like an achievement to have done.

Go early. The queue for the top cable car gets serious by 10am in good weather.

## Lunch — Swiss prices, Swiss quality

Be warned: lunch in Verbier costs roughly twice what it does in France. Build it into your budget rather than getting a shock on day one.

**Chez Dany** in La Marlenaz is the classic — a working farm restaurant, sleigh ride down in the dark after dinner, properly atmospheric. **Le Namaste** at the top of Savoleyres has the views and reliable Swiss-Italian food. **Le Mouton Noir** in the village does excellent simpler food at relatively saner prices.

For an experience: **La Marmotte** is on the way up to Croix de Coeur. Tiny, family-run, no menu, you eat what they're cooking. Worth the effort of finding.

## Après and after-dark

Verbier's après scene is famous and largely centred on the **Farinet** complex — the lounge bar, the after-ski stage, the late club. It's expensive, it's busy, and at peak weeks it's where everyone ends up.

For something quieter: **W Bar** at the W Hotel does serious cocktails in a grown-up setting. **Le Rouge** is the long-running locals' favourite, on the way to the home run, with consistently good music and a proper terrace.

Verbier's nightlife is genuinely among the best in the Alps if you're into that. If you're not, the village is small enough that you can avoid it easily.

## Non-skiing things worth doing

The **Verbier 3D Sculpture Park** at the top of the Médran lifts is a free, open-air contemporary art park. Unexpected and brilliant on a sunny day.

**Paragliding** from Les Ruinettes is the classic non-ski activity — the take-off is one of the best in Europe.

For families, the **Mini Verbier** play areas and **Moon Park** activities offer non-ski options that actually work for children.

## The honest reality

Verbier is expensive — not French Alps expensive, Swiss expensive. A week here costs more than the equivalent week in France, the food is dearer, and the lift pass adds up. You're paying for the mountain, the off-piste access, and a village with genuine character.

If you ski intermediate pistes and never venture off them, you'll have a great week in France for less money. If you want off-piste, freeride terrain, and a village with serious mountain culture, Verbier is worth what it costs.
