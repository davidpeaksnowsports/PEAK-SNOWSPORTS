There's a moment, usually about three days into a ski holiday, when you realise you should have started preparing weeks ago. Your legs are burning, your goggles fog up every run, and the lesson you booked turns out to be the wrong level. Sound familiar?

We've spent enough winters watching guests arrive in resort to know the patterns. The people who have the best week aren't necessarily the strongest skiers — they're the ones who prepared properly. Here's what actually matters.

## Get your legs in before you arrive

Skiing punishes the unprepared quadriceps like nothing else. Six weeks out, start doing wall-sits. Three sets of 45 seconds, three times a week. Add lunges and step-ups. If you do nothing else, do this.

The reason isn't just comfort — tired legs cause most ski injuries. By day three of a holiday, fatigue compounds, technique falls apart, and that's when knees go. A bit of conditioning before you arrive means you ski better, longer, and safer.

## Book your lessons before you book your accommodation

This sounds backwards, but hear us out. Good instructors in popular resorts get booked solid by October for peak weeks. By the time you've sorted flights and chalets in November, you're left with whoever's available — which often isn't the right fit.

Lock in lessons first, especially if you have specific needs (kids, mixed-ability groups, off-piste guiding). Then build the rest of the trip around them.

## The gear conversation nobody has

Renting in resort is fine. But there's a hierarchy: boots matter most, then skis, then poles (which barely matter at all). If you're going to splurge on one thing, get properly fitted boots — either bought at home or pre-booked at a quality resort fitter rather than the queue-on-arrival rental shop.

For clothing, the unsexy truth is that mid-layers and socks make more difference than your jacket. One good merino base layer and decent ski socks will transform your week.

## Sort the boring admin early

EHIC or GHIC card (if you're a UK or EU traveller), travel insurance that explicitly covers winter sports off-piste if relevant, lift pass pre-booking (often 10-15% cheaper than gate prices), and airport transfers. Resort transfers in particular sell out for Saturday changeovers — book them when you book your flights.

## The week before: small things that matter

Break in new boots by wearing them around the house for 30 minutes a day. Charge your action camera batteries. Download offline maps of the resort to your phone. Pack ibuprofen, blister plasters, and lip balm with SPF — the three things you'll definitely need and definitely forget.

And finally: arrive a day early if you possibly can. The difference between starting your first ski day jet-lagged versus rested is enormous. You'll ski better, learn faster, and enjoy it more.

The best ski holidays aren't about luck or expensive kit. They're about the unglamorous preparation that happens in October and November. Get those right, and the rest takes care of itself.
