We've taught a lot of families. The honeymooning couples come and go, the gap-year crews blur together, but families — families we remember. Mostly because family ski holidays sit on a knife edge between magical week and unmitigated disaster, and the difference often comes down to a handful of decisions made before you even arrive.

Here's what actually works.

## Pick the resort for the weakest skier, not the strongest

This is the single most common mistake. Dad wants Verbier, so the family goes to Verbier, and then the seven-year-old spends a week crying on a red run that's too steep while mum tries to keep up.

Choose a resort with genuinely excellent beginner terrain — wide blues, gentle greens, dedicated learner areas separate from the through-traffic. Strong skiers in the family will find more than enough to keep them entertained. Beginners in resorts designed for advanced skiers will not.

## Don't try to teach your own kids

We say this as parents and as instructors: it ends in tears, usually both yours and theirs. Children listen to instructors in ways they will never listen to their parents. Book proper lessons, drop them off, ski yourselves, come back at lunch. Everyone is happier.

For kids under six, look for instructors who specialise in teaching young children — it's a completely different skill from teaching adults. And consider whether group or private suits your child. Sociable confident kids thrive in groups. Anxious or younger children often do far better one-to-one.

## The lunch problem is the real problem

Adults can ski through lunch. Children cannot. Plan your day around a proper, sit-down lunch with actual food — not a snack on the chairlift. Energy crashes in cold kids happen fast, and once they've crashed, the afternoon is over.

Many families find a half-day ski morning, lunch, and a short afternoon works far better than trying to push through. The kids ski more total minutes because they're not exhausted, and nobody ends up in tears at 3pm.

## Layers, not big coats

Children overheat fast going up, then freeze fast standing in lift queues. The solution is layers — multiple thin layers they can add and remove — rather than one giant puffer jacket. Always pack a spare pair of gloves in your backpack. Wet gloves are the fastest route to a child wanting to go home.

## Build in a non-ski day

A six-day ski holiday with kids is too much. Plan one day off — go swimming, find an ice rink, visit a husky park, do anything that isn't skiing. Kids come back to the slopes on day five fresh and keen rather than dragging.

## Manage expectations, including your own

You're not going to ski the way you did pre-kids. You're going to ski less, ski slower, and ski easier terrain. That's not a bad holiday — it's a different holiday. The parents who accept this have a great time. The ones who fight it spend the week frustrated.

The best family ski holidays we see aren't the ones at the fanciest resorts with the priciest chalets. They're the ones where the parents made smart choices upfront, lowered the right expectations, and let the kids actually enjoy themselves. Get those right, and you'll be back next year.
